# Student guide

This is a game with **pretend money**. The companies are made up, nothing here is real, and it is not investment advice.

## Joining
1. Scan the QR code on the screen with your phone camera, or open the address shown and type the 6-digit **PIN**.
2. Choose a nickname (not your full name). No account or password.
3. Wait for your teacher to start. If you refresh or lose signal, just reopen the page: your game is saved.

## Each week
A row of six steps at the top shows where you are: read the news, explore companies, make decisions, finish trading, review the results, then the quiz and reflection. The green card tells you the next thing to do. Tap **How it works** any time to replay the introduction.

1. **Read the news.** Four stories come out each week: about a company, an industry, or the economy. Open each one and read the whole story. Headlines can leave out important details.
2. **Explore companies.** The **Companies** tab lists ten companies and a fund. Tap one to open its page: a price chart, a simple "at a glance" summary, and its news. Tap **Explore more company details** and the little **i** buttons to learn what each number means.
3. **Decide:** buy, sell, or hold. On a company page pick a number of whole shares and check the **order preview** ("You are buying 2 shares at $25 each. Total cost: $50. Cash remaining: $950."), then tap **Confirm purchase** or **Confirm sale**. You can only spend cash you have and sell shares you own, and the page tells you if an order cannot go through. Doing nothing is a valid choice.
4. **Finish trading for this week.** This does not place an order: your buys and sells already happened. It tells your teacher you are done. You can tap **Change my decisions** until trading closes.
5. **See the reaction.** Prices move based on the news, what investors expected, and a little chance. The reveal shows what drove each move.
6. **Quiz.** Three questions with an explanation after each answer. You only get one try per question, so think first. When your teacher ends the quiz, any unanswered questions close.
7. **Reflect.** See what affected your holdings, what your choices led to, and what to think about next week.

## Thinking like an investor
- Good news can still push a price down if investors expected even more. Look for what is *new* in a story.
- Ask what is good in a story **and** what is a worry. Which number matters more: revenue, profit margin, or cash?
- The same news can affect companies differently, depending on debt, customers, and business.
- Spreading money across different investments (diversification) lowers risk, but does not remove it.
- A lucky week is not skill. Judge a decision by its reasoning.

## Winning
The leaderboard ranks **portfolio value** (cash plus the value of your shares). Quiz points are separate. They show how well you understood investing, but they do not change your rank.

## Where to find things
- **My Portfolio:** your total, your cash, how much is invested, a chart against your $1,000 start, your investments, and your transactions.
- **Companies:** every investment, with its risk level in plain English.
- **News:** every story, with a "Think like an investor" box on each one.
- **Leaderboard:** who is ahead.

# Weekly quiz bank and answer key (TEACHER ONLY)

Each week has 5 questions: 3 supplied core questions (marked *core*) and 2 extra ones. Every game asks 3 of the 5 each week, chosen at random from the game's seed.
Answer order is shuffled separately for every student, so the letters below are the **bank order**, not what a student sees.
Students are shown the correct answer and explanation only after they submit each question. Each question can be answered once.
Edit questions in `server/data/quiz.js` (see [CUSTOMIZING.md](CUSTOMIZING.md)).

## Week 1: Stock market fundamentals

*Concept:* Share prices move on expectations and the full financial picture, not on a single headline number.

**1. A student buys 10 shares of a company for $20 per share. The share price rises to $24. Ignoring fees and taxes, what is the student’s unrealized gain?** *(core)*

- A. $4
- B. $24
- C. $40 **(correct)**
- D. $240

*Explanation:* The gain per share is $24 minus $20, or $4. Ten shares produce an unrealized gain of $40. It remains unrealized until the shares are sold.

**2. A company announces record revenue, but its share price falls after the announcement. Which explanation is most reasonable?** *(core)*

- A. A stock must fall whenever revenue increases.
- B. Investors may have expected even stronger results or been concerned about profitability. **(correct)**
- C. Revenue has no relationship to a company’s business performance.
- D. Every investor must have sold their shares.

*Explanation:* Market reactions depend on expectations and other financial information, not just whether one metric increased.

**3. What does owning an ordinary share generally represent?** *(core)*

- A. Lending money directly to the government.
- B. Owning a portion of a company. **(correct)**
- C. Receiving a guaranteed annual payment.
- D. Having a claim to a fixed interest rate.

*Explanation:* Shares generally represent ownership in a company, although the rights attached to shares vary.

**4. A share price falls from $40 to $34. By what percentage did it fall?** *(extra)*

- A. 6%
- B. 15% **(correct)**
- C. 17.6%
- D. 34%

*Explanation:* The fall is $6. Dividing $6 by the starting price of $40 gives 0.15, or 15%.

**5. What is a ticker symbol?** *(extra)*

- A. A short code that identifies a company’s shares on an exchange. **(correct)**
- B. The price at which a company first sold shares.
- C. A guarantee that a company will pay dividends.
- D. The number of shares a student owns.

*Explanation:* A ticker is a short code, such as the ones used in this game, that identifies the shares of a listed company.

## Week 2: ETFs and diversification

*Concept:* A diversified fund can reduce the damage from one company’s problems, but it cannot remove the risk of the whole market falling.

**1. What does ETF stand for?** *(core)*

- A. Electronic Trading Fund
- B. Exchange-Traded Fund **(correct)**
- C. Equity Transfer Facility
- D. Estimated Treasury Fund

*Explanation:* An exchange-traded fund is an investment fund whose shares trade on an exchange.

**2. A student invests $500 in a fund holding shares in 100 different companies. What is the main potential benefit compared with investing the entire $500 in one company?** *(core)*

- A. The fund guarantees a positive return.
- B. Diversification can reduce the impact of problems at a single company. **(correct)**
- C. The fund cannot lose value.
- D. The fund eliminates all market risk.

*Explanation:* Diversification can reduce company-specific risk, but a broadly invested fund can still lose value when the overall market declines.

**3. Two ETFs have similar holdings. ETF A charges a 0.10% annual expense ratio, while ETF B charges 0.80%. Assuming their gross investment performance is identical, which statement is correct?** *(core)*

- A. ETF B should always outperform because its fee is higher.
- B. ETF A will generally leave more of the investment return with the investor. **(correct)**
- C. ETF A guarantees higher returns.
- D. Fees matter only when an ETF loses money.

*Explanation:* Lower ongoing fees leave more of the fund’s gross return with investors, all else being equal.

**4. What is the aim of an index-tracking ETF?** *(extra)*

- A. To beat every other fund every year.
- B. To match the performance of a market index, before fees. **(correct)**
- C. To guarantee a minimum return.
- D. To hold only the single best-performing company.

*Explanation:* An index-tracking ETF tries to follow the performance of an index by holding the same or similar investments.

**5. A student’s only investment is an ETF that tracks a broad market index. The overall market falls 10%. What is most likely to happen?** *(extra)*

- A. The ETF will probably fall too. **(correct)**
- B. The ETF will rise because it is diversified.
- C. The ETF will stay exactly the same.
- D. The ETF will be removed from the exchange.

*Explanation:* Diversification reduces company-specific risk, but it does not protect against a decline in the whole market.

## Week 3: Risk and return

*Concept:* Higher potential returns come with higher risk. Decisions should weigh both, and a price drop alone is not a reason to act.

**1. A student holds shares in five companies, all in the same industry. What is an important limitation of this portfolio?** *(core)*

- A. It cannot generate dividends.
- B. It may remain vulnerable to an industry-wide downturn. **(correct)**
- C. It is automatically diversified across the entire market.
- D. It cannot experience short-term price changes.

*Explanation:* Holding several companies does not eliminate concentration risk when they depend on the same industry.

**2. An investment has a possible high return but also a meaningful chance of a large loss. Which statement is most accurate?** *(core)*

- A. A higher possible return guarantees a higher actual return.
- B. Risk should be evaluated alongside potential return, time horizon, and ability to absorb losses. **(correct)**
- C. High-risk investments are always better for young investors.
- D. A diversified portfolio cannot lose money.

*Explanation:* Investment decisions require considering both the potential reward and the risks involved.

**3. A student’s portfolio falls 15% in one week. Which response best reflects sound investing principles?** *(core)*

- A. Immediately invest all remaining cash in the worst-performing stock.
- B. Evaluate the original investment reasoning, new information, diversification, and goals before deciding what to do. **(correct)**
- C. Assume the portfolio will recover by the following week.
- D. Sell everything automatically whenever prices fall.

*Explanation:* A price decline alone does not determine the best action. Decisions should reflect evidence, risk tolerance, and objectives.

**4. Which portfolio is the most diversified?** *(extra)*

- A. All the money in one technology company.
- B. Five different technology companies.
- C. Companies from several industries plus a broad market ETF. **(correct)**
- D. Two companies that sell to the same customers.

*Explanation:* Spreading money across different industries and a broad fund reduces dependence on any single company or sector.

**5. What does volatility describe?** *(extra)*

- A. How much and how quickly an investment’s price changes. **(correct)**
- B. How many shares a company has issued.
- C. Whether a company pays a dividend.
- D. The fee charged by a fund.

*Explanation:* Volatility describes the size and speed of price changes. A more volatile investment has a wider range of possible outcomes, both up and down.

## Week 4: Dividends and company financials

*Concept:* Revenue, profit, cash flow, and dividends tell different parts of the story. One strong figure does not guarantee a strong company.

**1. A company reports higher revenue but lower net income. What is a plausible explanation?** *(core)*

- A. Its expenses may have increased faster than its revenue. **(correct)**
- B. Revenue and profit must always move in the same direction.
- C. It must have issued a dividend.
- D. Its share price must rise.

*Explanation:* Higher sales do not guarantee higher profit when operating expenses, interest costs, or other costs rise.

**2. What is a dividend?** *(core)*

- A. A guaranteed increase in share price.
- B. A distribution of company value to eligible shareholders, often in cash. **(correct)**
- C. A fee paid by every investor to a stock exchange.
- D. A loan that must always be repaid by shareholders.

*Explanation:* Companies may distribute part of their earnings or other available funds to shareholders, but dividends are not guaranteed.

**3. A company pays a dividend, but its share price decreases by a similar amount on the ex-dividend date. What should students understand?** *(core)*

- A. Dividends always create an immediate profit.
- B. The share price may adjust to reflect the distribution, so the dividend alone does not guarantee a gain. **(correct)**
- C. The company has necessarily become more profitable.
- D. Every shareholder has lost the full value of the dividend.

*Explanation:* Dividends are one component of investment return, and share-price adjustments can offset some or all of the apparent benefit.

**4. What does net income measure?** *(extra)*

- A. The profit left after all of a company’s expenses. **(correct)**
- B. The total amount of cash in the company’s bank accounts.
- C. The money a company raised by selling shares.
- D. Total sales before any costs.

*Explanation:* Net income is a company’s profit after subtracting expenses such as operating costs, interest, and taxes from revenue.

**5. A company’s free cash flow falls, but it raises its dividend. Which question should an investor ask?** *(extra)*

- A. Can the company sustain the higher dividend from the cash it generates? **(correct)**
- B. Does the higher dividend guarantee a higher share price?
- C. Is free cash flow irrelevant to dividends?
- D. Will the company stop reporting results?

*Explanation:* Dividends are paid from cash. If cash generation weakens, a higher dividend may be harder to maintain.

## Week 5: Interest rates and economic conditions

*Concept:* Economic developments affect companies differently depending on their debt, customers, and business models.

**1. A company has substantial debt with variable interest rates. Interest rates rise. Why could this affect the company?** *(core)*

- A. Its borrowing costs may increase and reduce profits. **(correct)**
- B. Its revenue must double.
- C. Its shares become government-guaranteed.
- D. Its debt automatically disappears.

*Explanation:* Higher borrowing costs can reduce cash flow and profitability for companies with variable-rate debt or refinancing needs.

**2. A retailer reports strong sales, but customers are increasingly using credit to cover everyday expenses. What potential concern might investors consider?** *(core)*

- A. Consumer demand and future spending could weaken if household finances deteriorate. **(correct)**
- B. The retailer’s sales are guaranteed to grow indefinitely.
- C. Credit use eliminates all business risk.
- D. Higher household debt guarantees higher corporate profits.

*Explanation:* Current sales figures may not fully capture the sustainability of consumer demand.

**3. Why might two companies react differently to the same increase in interest rates?** *(core)*

- A. Interest rates affect all companies identically.
- B. Their debt levels, financing needs, customers, and business models may differ. **(correct)**
- C. Interest rates affect only banks.
- D. Share prices are unrelated to economic conditions.

*Explanation:* Economic developments affect businesses differently depending on their circumstances.

**4. When a central bank raises interest rates, what is a typical effect on borrowing?** *(extra)*

- A. New or variable-rate borrowing becomes more expensive. **(correct)**
- B. All existing loans are cancelled.
- C. Borrowing becomes free for businesses.
- D. Banks must stop lending.

*Explanation:* Higher policy rates generally raise the interest charged on new loans and on loans whose rates adjust over time.

**5. Why might a bank’s profit benefit from higher interest rates?** *(extra)*

- A. It may earn more on loans relative to what it pays on deposits, though defaults can also rise. **(correct)**
- B. Higher rates always make every loan safer.
- C. Banks do not charge interest.
- D. Higher rates reduce the bank’s costs to zero.

*Explanation:* Banks can earn a wider gap between lending and deposit rates, but higher rates can also strain borrowers and raise defaults.

## Week 6: Valuation and investor expectations

*Concept:* Prices reflect expectations. A good result can disappoint, and a ratio like P/E needs context before it says anything.

**1. Company A earns $5 per share and trades at $50 per share. Company B earns $5 per share and trades at $100 per share. Based only on these figures, which statement is correct?** *(core)*

- A. Company A has a price-to-earnings ratio of 10, while Company B has a ratio of 20. **(correct)**
- B. Company B is automatically the better investment.
- C. Company A is guaranteed to outperform.
- D. Both companies have a price-to-earnings ratio of 5.

*Explanation:* Price-to-earnings ratio equals share price divided by earnings per share. The ratio alone does not establish which investment is better.

**2. A technology company reports 20% revenue growth, but its share price falls because investors expected 35% growth. What concept does this demonstrate?** *(core)*

- A. Market prices can reflect expectations as well as reported results. **(correct)**
- B. Revenue growth always causes a price increase.
- C. Investors cannot react to company announcements.
- D. A company’s reported revenue is irrelevant to its business.

*Explanation:* Results can be positive in isolation but disappoint relative to expectations already reflected in the share price.

**3. A company has a high price-to-earnings ratio compared with similar businesses. What is the most reasonable conclusion?** *(core)*

- A. Its shares are guaranteed to fall.
- B. Investors may be pricing in stronger future growth, but the valuation also warrants scrutiny. **(correct)**
- C. It is automatically more profitable than every competitor.
- D. Its financial results no longer matter.

*Explanation:* Valuation ratios need context, including expected growth, risk, profitability, and industry comparisons.

**4. A company earns $4 per share and its shares trade at $60. What is its price-to-earnings ratio?** *(extra)*

- A. 4
- B. 15 **(correct)**
- C. 60
- D. 240

*Explanation:* P/E equals price divided by earnings per share: $60 divided by $4 equals 15.

**5. A stock’s P/E ratio is far above its industry average. Which question is most reasonable to ask?** *(extra)*

- A. Does the company’s expected growth justify the higher price? **(correct)**
- B. Does this prove the stock will fall next week?
- C. Does this prove the company is the best in its industry?
- D. Can the ratio be ignored?

*Explanation:* A high P/E can reflect high growth expectations or overvaluation. Investors should look at growth, risk, and profitability to judge which.

## Week 7: Portfolio management and trading decisions

*Concept:* Position size, concentration, and reading the full story matter as much as picking investments.

**1. A student has $200 in cash and wants to purchase eight shares priced at $28 each. Ignoring fees, what is the result?** *(core)*

- A. The student can buy eight shares and retain $24.
- B. The student can buy seven shares and retain $4. **(correct)**
- C. The student can buy six shares and retain $32.
- D. The student cannot buy any shares.

*Explanation:* Eight shares cost $224, which exceeds the available cash. Seven shares cost $196, leaving $4.

**2. A student’s largest holding represents 70% of the portfolio. What is the main concern?** *(core)*

- A. The student cannot earn dividends.
- B. The portfolio is highly concentrated and exposed to the performance of one holding. **(correct)**
- C. The portfolio cannot increase in value.
- D. A large holding always produces a loss.

*Explanation:* Concentration increases exposure to the risks associated with a single investment.

**3. A student sells a stock immediately after a negative headline without reading the full report. What is the most important weakness in that decision?** *(core)*

- A. Selling is always a mistake.
- B. The decision may be based on incomplete information rather than the underlying business implications. **(correct)**
- C. Investors should never read company news.
- D. Negative headlines always indicate a buying opportunity.

*Explanation:* Headlines can omit important context. Investors should examine the available evidence before making decisions.

**4. A student has $500 in cash and shares cost $64 each. How many whole shares can the student buy?** *(extra)*

- A. 7 **(correct)**
- B. 8
- C. 9
- D. 6

*Explanation:* Seven shares cost $448 and leave $52. Eight shares would cost $512, which is more than $500.

**5. Which is a sound reason to reduce a position in a portfolio?** *(extra)*

- A. It has grown to a large share of the portfolio and adds concentration risk. **(correct)**
- B. Its price rose once last week.
- C. A friend said to sell.
- D. The ticker symbol is hard to remember.

*Explanation:* Trimming a position that has become very large can reduce dependence on a single investment.

## Week 8: Long-term investing and final review

*Concept:* Compounding rewards patience, benchmarks give context, and short results are heavily influenced by luck.

**1. An investment earns an average annual return over several years. Why can compound growth be powerful?** *(core)*

- A. Returns can generate additional returns when gains remain invested. **(correct)**
- B. Returns are guaranteed to be positive every year.
- C. Compounding eliminates investment risk.
- D. It prevents share prices from fluctuating.

*Explanation:* Compounding occurs when investment gains remain invested and contribute to future growth. Actual market returns vary.

**2. A student finishes the simulation with the highest portfolio value after making several concentrated bets that happened to succeed. What is the most balanced assessment?** *(core)*

- A. The student has proven that concentrated investing is always best.
- B. The final result matters, but decision quality, risk taken, diversification, and the role of luck should also be evaluated. **(correct)**
- C. The student is guaranteed to outperform in real markets.
- D. Investment research is unnecessary when the final return is positive.

*Explanation:* A successful outcome does not necessarily mean the process was sound. Random outcomes can reward risky decisions in a short simulation.

**3. What is the strongest reason to compare investment performance with an appropriate benchmark?** *(core)*

- A. It guarantees that the portfolio will beat the market.
- B. It helps assess performance relative to a relevant comparison rather than looking only at the portfolio’s raw return. **(correct)**
- C. It eliminates all uncertainty.
- D. It proves that every losing investment was a mistake.

*Explanation:* A relevant benchmark provides context for evaluating performance, although differences in risk and portfolio composition must also be considered.

**4. Which is a reasonable benchmark for a portfolio of diversified stocks?** *(extra)*

- A. A broad market index fund covering similar companies. **(correct)**
- B. The price of one random stock.
- C. The portfolio’s own result from last week.
- D. A savings account with a guaranteed rate, in every case.

*Explanation:* A good benchmark resembles the portfolio’s holdings and risk, such as a broad index of similar companies.

**5. Why is an eight-week result an unreliable measure of investing skill?** *(extra)*

- A. Short periods are heavily influenced by chance and by the timing of news. **(correct)**
- B. Skill cannot exist in investing.
- C. Eight weeks is the legal minimum for measuring performance.
- D. Only the final day matters.

*Explanation:* Over short periods, luck and timing can outweigh skill. Long-term results are a better guide.
